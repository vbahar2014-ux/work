⚡ Pattern of the Day
Yesterday's regulatory bet became law overnight — and the binding constraints on enterprise AI moved from capability to control, power and portability.

Yesterday the Digital Omnibus was a provisional political agreement, and the call was to treat the Annex III deferral as a bet. It resolved: Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 was published in the Official Journal on 24 July and entered into force 27 July — three days later instead of the usual twenty, to settle the rules before the AI Act's 2 August application date. The high-risk Annex III deferral to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex I to 2 Aug 2028) is now legally certain, a new Article 5 prohibition on AI "nudifiers" / non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM is added, and GPAI enforcement + Article 50 disclosure remain live from 2 August.

The physical and commercial layers hardened in parallel. New York became the first US state to freeze large (≥50 MW) data-center permits — following Texas's ERCOT audit; Q2 earnings showed AI capex converting into record cloud revenue (Azure past $100B/yr, Google Cloud +63% YoY); and AWS + Google published an open multicloud-networking interoperability spec that chips at egress lock-in. The through-line: the question is shifting from "can we get the capability" to "can we site it, afford it, exit it, and prove it's compliant."

For Group Two moves this cycle: (1) rephase the Annex III high-risk readiness plan to the now-legally-certain 2 Dec 2027 date — freeing near-term capacity — while keeping GPAI and Article 50 obligations live in production and screening every system against the new Article 5 prohibitions; and (2) make portability the design constant — fold the AWS×Google open interconnect spec into landing-zone planning and keep multicloud exit-portability first-class.
