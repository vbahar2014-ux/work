⚡ Pattern of the Day
Two threads harden. The power ceiling escalates from an operator warning into a political fight — while the agent control plane stops being a product and becomes a published standard.

A day after adding its "capacity advisory," PJM's reliability miss drew alarms from FERC commissioners and federal officials floating a break-up of the largest US grid. The Dec 2025 capacity auction cleared at a record $333.44/MW-day — roughly 10× the 2024 level — after falling ~6.6 GW short of the reliability target; data centres are ~94% of projected load growth, and ratepayers absorb ~$1.4B in added capacity costs from June 2026. Power is no longer just a siting constraint — it is structural and political risk.

In parallel, governance crosses from product to standard. Microsoft's Build stack ships an open trust stack — the Agent Control Specification (deterministic allow/deny at five lifecycle checkpoints) plus Agent 365 over Entra/Defender/Purview and MAI models carrying machine-readable "trust rubrics" — while the EU publishes the final Code of Practice on marking AI-generated content, the technical scaffold for Article 50. AI Act clock at ~37 days.

For Group Two calls. Keep the grid-reliability / power-availability sensitivity in US capacity planning and contracts — PJM's risk is now structural and political, not weather-driven — and treat the now-final Article 50 marking Code as the concrete spec to build the AI-generated-content inventory and marking rollout against, ~37 days out.
