⚡ Pattern of the Day
Agent governance stops being a bolt-on and gets absorbed into the platform layer.

Last cycle the agent stack's three missing pieces arrived as separate products — identity from a security vendor (Cequence), a settlement rail from a network (Cloudflare), a power mandate from a regulator (FERC). This cycle the identity piece moves inside the platform: Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform reaches GA with dedicated Agent Identity credentials, least-privilege scoping, multi-day state and full operation logging — and Gemini Spark begins operating the desktop version of Chrome under logged-in sessions.

So the identity/audit primitive AG has been weighing as a buy or a build is now also a default of the cloud AG already runs on — convenient and dangerous in the same breath, because platform-native identity lowers the effort to govern agents but raises the cost of leaving. Around it, regulation is operational (EU AI Act enforcement confirmed ~16 days in; the Code-of-Practice presumption turns signatory status into a checkable conformity signal), and power stayed the binding constraint as the gap widened — US data-center demand ran 23→42 GW, pushing regional mega-builds (AWS's $48B India bet) and novel siting (floating data centers, Batam, NEC-asset roll-ups).

For Group Two moves this cycle: (1) re-scope the agent control-plane decision to include platform-native identity (Google Agent Identity) as a third option alongside Cequence (buy) and build — and weigh the lock-in explicitly — before the next production rollout; and (2) confirm which of AG's GPAI suppliers are Code-of-Practice signatories and record it as a conformity factor in vendor governance while operating GPAI + Article 50 in production.
