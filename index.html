⚡ Pattern of the Day
The threat is migrating inward — from the internet edge you patch fast to the installed base you must know, own and govern.

For a fortnight this brief tracked the compression of the exploit window on the trust fabric — build pipeline, AI gateway, remote-access edge, hypervisor, and last week the browser. Today the lens turns to the estate Group already runs: a Microsoft Exchange auth-bypass (CVE-2026-62911) with public exploit code and ~21,900 servers still exposed is a reminder that on-prem, internet-facing infrastructure is weaponised on the same days-not-weeks clock — and it lands the day before 8 Sept Patch Tuesday while three earlier exposures still sit open: the Chrome V8 zero-day (deadline 18 Sept), the lapsed 2 Sept KEV batch (Artifactory / LiteLLM / SonicWall), and the live vCenter campaign.

The same inward turn shows on the strategic axis. Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard extends agents from "acting on data" to "acting on infrastructure" — a governance problem that arrives before the capability is widespread, exactly when Group can still set agent identity, deny-by-default runtime and credential isolation as preconditions rather than retrofits. And the supply side — Micron doubling HBM, data centres taking ~70% of 2026 memory, builds slipping to 2028 — means the physical substrate of any Group-owned AI platform must be secured years ahead, not quarters.

For Group A shift of attention, not architecture: last week was "patch the edge faster"; this week is "know your own installed base." Confirm every on-prem/hybrid Exchange server is patched for CVE-2026-62911 now and fold it into tomorrow's Patch Tuesday; keep the three carryover Acts closed under assume-compromise; pull agent governance forward ahead of Fable-class agents-on-infra and Dreamforce (15–17 Sept, ~8 days); and hold the sovereign-AI (Microsoft EU Data Boundary / EU Cloud & AI Act), memory-supercycle, EO 14420, PJM 30 Sept auction and 2 Dec EU AI Act marking (~86 days) watchpoints.

📡 Radar — early & longer-horizon signals
N-able N-central — fresh access-control-bypass PoC (CVE-2026-86207)
Huntress published a proof-of-concept on 5 September for a flaw in N-able N-central that bypasses access controls to create unauthorized administrative accounts, weeks after a distinct N-central auth-bypass (CVE-2026-18577) was KEV-listed with confirmed customer compromises. RMM/MSP platforms are a high-value pivot into managed endpoints; relevant if Group or its managed-service providers run N-central. Next trigger: confirmed in-the-wild exploitation or KEV addition.
WordPress Super Forms + Elementor Pro under active exploitation
Attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-14894 in the Super Forms plugin (unauthenticated file upload → RCE) alongside an Elementor Pro flaw, putting public WordPress sites at risk of takeover. Relevant to any Group marketing/brand web property on WordPress. Next trigger: a mass-exploitation campaign or a named victim.
Cloudflare pushes AI-native netsec and flips agentic-access defaults (15 Sept)
Cloudflare's Vulnerability Discovery and Remediation pairs OpenAI Daybreak security models with its network traffic data to find, prioritise and fix flaws; separately, from 15 September its default for ad-supported pages allows search but blocks AI training and agent access. A content-access, agent-governance and AI-native-defence signal for any Group web property or crawling agent. Next trigger: adoption data or a publisher/agent dispute over the default.
PJM 6.8 GW reliability backstop auction runs 30 Sept–21 Oct
Following FERC's 3 Sept comment-window close, PJM will auction up to 6.8 GW under its "bring-your-own-power or face state-approved curtailment" regime (≥50 MW single-POI, post-1 June 2027 large-load definition; 12 Oct requested effective date). The pricing/eligibility signal for any PJM-footprint Group capacity. Next trigger: FERC's ruling or auction clearing prices.
Data-center capacity slippage — 30–50% of planned 2026 builds slip to 2028
Omdia/industry analysis holds that chip, power and long-lead-item scarcity will push 30–50% of planned 2026 data-center capacity to 2028; capex at the largest operators nears ~$750B in 2026. Reinforces Group's lead-time and phasing assumptions for any new capacity — and compounds the memory-supply premium above. Next trigger: a named hyperscaler build delay or a further lead-time step-out.
