⚡ Pattern of the Day
The bottleneck keeps moving down the stack.

For weeks this brief has tracked AG's build-out constraint migrating from the thing everyone watches — GPU allocation — to the things nobody puts on a board agenda. First it was power: interconnection queues, transformer lead times, and PJM's curtailment rule, whose FERC comment window closes tomorrow. This cycle the constraint moved one layer further, into memory: with ~70% of 2026 DRAM output diverted to AI data centres, Samsung has raised memory prices up to 60% since September, DDR5 contract pricing has more than doubled, and the big three refuse multi-year agreements in favour of quarterly step-ups while 2026 capacity sells out.

The pattern is consistent: the scarce, expensive, long-lead inputs are the mundane ones, priced and allocated on clocks that do not wait for an architecture review. Against that backdrop the VMware story gained its missing number — VCF 9 lists at ~$350/core/year with Private AI bundled at no extra cost — so the sovereign-AI landing-zone case is finally modellable, but the vCenter control plane it runs on stays under a live RCE campaign, so the platform-economics decision and the platform-hardening decision remain the same decision.

For Group Stop treating power and memory as procurement footnotes and start treating them as first-order architecture constraints with their own deadlines. Four moves this cycle: (1) lock a PJM self-supply/curtailment clause before tomorrow's 3 Sept close; (2) re-baseline server-refresh and capacity budgets to the new DRAM reality and secure allocation now; (3) keep hunting and patching the vCenter and NetScaler/PaperCut estates under assume-compromise; (4) model the VCF-9 ~$350/core economics into the Private AI Cloud review — while holding the EU AI Act 2 Dec marking schedule.

suggested actions — assign owner & date
01
Infrastructure & Data Centers

Lock a self-supply / curtailment clause into any PJM-footprint capacity commitment and add a behind-the-meter test to capacity diligence — FERC's comment/intervention window on PJM's two filings ($20B reliability backstop + "bring-your-own-power or face state-approved curtailment," filed 13 Aug) closes 3 September — tomorrow — ahead of the 12 October effective date. A "New Large Load" is ≥50 MW at a single Point of Interconnection entering service after 1 June 2027, and any demand not backed by qualifying new capacity may be curtailed before other pre-emergency measures; the Reliability Backstop auction begins September 2026 against a ~60 GW shortfall

Suggested action · With one day left to comment, treat the intervention window as now — decide and require a self-supply/curtailment clause in any PJM-footprint colo or capacity commitment, confirm which state can order a load cut, and add a behind-the-meter-capability test to capacity diligence. Consequence of inaction: curtailment becomes a commercial term AG cannot renegotiate, and a US state could order a load cut on AG-footprint compute with no contractual self-supply hedge.
Owner: Infrastructure Strategy / Capacity PlanningBy when: before 3 Sept comment close (tomorrow)
02
Infrastructure & Data Centers

Re-baseline server-refresh and capacity budgets to the new DRAM reality and secure memory allocation now — Samsung has raised memory prices up to 60% since September (a 32GB DDR5 module $149 → $239), DDR5 contract pricing is up >100%, Samsung/SK Hynix/Micron are refusing 2–3-year agreements in favour of quarterly step-ups, and SK Hynix reports HBM/DRAM/NAND "essentially sold out" for 2026 with ~70% of DRAM output going to AI data centres. A 256GB DDR5 ECC RDIMM refresh that budgeted $800–1,200 in early 2025 now faces $2,000–3,500; elevated pricing is projected to persist through 2027

Suggested action · Re-baseline refresh/capacity budgets to current memory pricing, secure allocation for near-term builds now, and factor quarterly (not annual) price escalation into TCO for any on-prem AI landing zone. Consequence of inaction: capacity and refresh plans built on 2025 memory prices are materially under-budgeted; deferring orders means a higher price next quarter — or no allocation at all.
Owner: Infrastructure Sourcing / Procurement + Capacity PlanningBy when: this procurement cycle (before next quarterly step-up)
03
Governance, Cyber & Regulatory Watch

Keep the vCenter estate patched and threat-hunted for CVE-2026-59310 — the critical directory-traversal flaw in the vCenter Syslog server (CVSS 9.8) gives unauthenticated attackers RCE. Broadcom fixed it 29 July; mass exploitation began ~3 Aug; researchers track 361 compromised IPs across 47 countries (US, France, Iran, Turkey heaviest). On CISA KEV since 18 Aug (federal remediation due 21 Aug, lapsed). Post-exploitation installs a malicious cron job + the reverse_ssh framework for firewall-evading C2, then pivots to Babuk-derived ransomware on ESXi; patching alone may not remediate an already-compromised host

Suggested action · Hunt the vCenter estate for reverse_ssh, anomalous cron jobs/root processes and Syslog-path-traversal indicators; confirm every appliance is patched; restrict management-plane exposure; treat any appliance unpatched since July as potentially compromised — and require a vCenter hardening/hunt sign-off as a precondition of any AI landing zone built on VMware. Consequence of inaction: a root foothold on the hypervisor control plane — the highest-value pivot in the estate — enabling estate-wide credential theft, ESXi ransomware, or dormant espionage persistence.
Owner: Security Operations (Threat Hunt) + Virtualization / Platform InfrastructureBy when: immediately (KEV deadline lapsed 21 Aug)

📡 Radar — early & longer-horizon signals
CI/CD & dev-toolchain RCE cluster widens
JetBrains TeamCity CVE-2026-63077 (CVSS 9.8 unauth RCE, on CISA KEV since 5 Aug) and Gitea CVE-2026-60004 (CVSS 9.8, KEV since 25 Aug) are both under active exploitation, exposing build artifacts, stored credentials and downstream pipelines — the same soft-underbelly pattern as the LiteLLM PyPI compromise. The build pipeline and self-hosted developer infrastructure are becoming a primary enterprise entry point; AG should inventory internet-facing CI/CD and repo servers. Next trigger: a named CI/CD-origin breach or a fresh dev-tool KEV addition.
Dreamforce 2026 (15–17 Sept) puts the "agentic enterprise" on trial
Agentforce across CRM/ERP/Data Cloud/Slack, 1,600+ sessions, with governance and integration architecture the urgent theme for leaders past the pilot stage. A near-term reference point for governed multi-agent orchestration patterns AG can study. Next trigger: Agentforce 360 governance/architecture detail at the keynote.
Grid interconnection queue tops 2,100 GW; transformers must scale ~6× by 2030
US interconnection requests now exceed total grid capacity, and transformer demand is projected to rise from ~1,500 to >9,000 units/year by decade's end, with the US data-center electrical-equipment market growing from ~$20B (2026) toward $65B (2030). The physical constraint behind PJM's curtailment rule — long-lead electrical gear gates delivery regardless of GPU or budget. Next trigger: a named AG-region interconnection delay or a further transformer lead-time extension.
Cloud outages settle into "the new normal"
Recent 2026 incidents include an Azure portal issue (14 Aug), an AWS partial outage (20 Aug) and a GCP us-west1 incident (20 Aug, ~3h40m), reinforcing Forrester's projection of multiple multi-day hyperscaler outages this year. Reinforces AG's multi-region / graceful-degradation posture for cloud-hosted critical workloads. Next trigger: a us-east-1-scale event in a region AG depends on.
Switchvox CVE-2026-9586 (CVSS 9.3) — unauth SQLi→RCE on enterprise VoIP
An unauthenticated SQL-injection-to-RCE flaw in Sangoma Switchvox is being exploited in the wild. A reminder that unified-communications/telephony appliances are part of the exposed edge; AG should confirm any Switchvox/UC platform is patched. Next trigger: a mass-scan campaign or a CISA KEV addition.
