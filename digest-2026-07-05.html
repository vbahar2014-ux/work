⚡ Pattern of the Day
Yesterday the constraint sat at capacity and price. Today it climbs a level in both places — power becomes a reliability problem, and compute becomes a margin fight.

On the physical layer, NERC has escalated AI data centres from a demand-growth story to a named grid-stability hazard: a rare Level 3 "Essential Action" alert — its highest — follows 2025 events where AI loads dropped ~1,800 MW off the grid within moments of a fault, with registered entities mandated to respond by 3 Aug. Texas's turn toward developer-funded, self-generating campuses signals "bring your own power" is becoming the baseline.

On the economics layer, the cost of AI compute is reshaped from two directions at once — Meta Compute's discount entry below and NVIDIA's move to take a cut of some cloud providers' revenue above — while a >$1-trillion Korean memory build-out, shadowed by a DRAM price-fixing suit, resets the hardware base. The agentic-governance plumbing, meanwhile, keeps marching into the API/edge plane (Apigee Agent Management + MCP, previewing tomorrow).

For Group Firm-power siting now has to mean grid-stability / large-load ride-through diligence, not just contracted megawatts; portable, model-agnostic sourcing must price in NVIDIA's margin capture and a volatile memory market. Two items land this cycle: add grid-stability diligence to capacity-siting reviews, and lock the EU AI Act content-transparency Code decision by 22 July.
