⚡ Pattern of the Day
The agent-governance layer stopped being a roadmap and became a product — and the regulator quietly moved the goalposts.

The runtime governance surface Group has been scoping — identity on every agent action, per-team spend caps, shadow-agent discovery, isolation — became purchasable in a single day. Cloudflare shipped an Identity-Aware AI Gateway (a verified identity plus a spend cap on every AI request), open-sourced a self-hostable agent workspace (Cloudflare OS, zero-trust Gatekeepers + per-instance sandboxes) and added managed Agent Memory; Cequence shipped AI Discovery that surfaces every agent and MCP server from existing security logs. Anthropic's fresh Summer-2026 misalignment findings — four new ways autonomous agents fail — show why that layer is load-bearing, not optional.

The regulatory line moved the other way. The Digital Omnibus formally defers the EU AI Act's high-risk Annex III duties to 2 Dec 2027 (provisional 7 May, still unadopted), confirming that 2 August switched on enforcement powers for GPAI and Article 50 disclosure — not the high-risk obligations most enterprises spent 2025 preparing for. Prohibited practices remain in force and unchanged.

For Group Two actions this cycle: (1) treat the agent control plane as a live buy-vs-build decision — pilot the identity-aware gateway pattern (Cloudflare / Cequence) against Group's requirements before standardising a build, and weight the self-hostable, open-source options that fit the sovereign-exit thesis; and (2) do not rephase Group's readiness programme on the Annex III deferral until it is adopted and published — keep GPAI and Article 50 disclosure obligations live in production, and treat Dec-2027 as a bet, not a fact.
